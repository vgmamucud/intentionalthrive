There's a style of financial content that looks flawless. Big numbers, calm confidence, a story with no rough edges.

I understand its appeal. I also don't trust it, and I don't think you should either.

## What honest looks like to me

It means saying what a product can't do alongside what it can. It means admitting when something is a trade-off rather than a bargain. It means telling you when the answer is "not yet," or "not this," or "you don't need this at all."

It also means being plain about my own position: I work in financial services. I earn from helping people with insurance. You deserve to know that, and to weigh what I say accordingly.

## Why it matters in this industry

Money decisions carry long consequences. A premium you can't keep paying, a product you didn't understand, a plan sold on a hopeful illustration: these cost people years. The best protection against them is clear language and honest limits.

## The rules I try to hold

**Say what it costs.** Including what it costs to leave.

**Separate fact from projection.** Guaranteed is guaranteed. Projected is a guess with a nice chart.

**Don't use fear.** Real stakes are enough.

**Let people say no.** A good decision often begins with the freedom to walk away.

## What I'm still learning

Plain language is harder than it sounds. It's tempting to fall back on jargon when I'm unsure, or to soften a point so it won't land badly. I'll get it wrong sometimes. When I do, I'd rather correct it publicly than hide it.

## What I'd ask of you

Hold me to this. Question what you read here. Compare it against other sources, and check any claim that matters to your decisions.

If this space helps you decide something more clearly, including deciding against something I mentioned, then it's doing its job.
