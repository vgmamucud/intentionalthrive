Things accumulate. Clothes, gadgets, subscriptions, commitments, even apps. Each addition is small and sensible, and together they crowd your home, your budget and your attention.

A simple rule slows it: **one in, one out.**

## How it works

Before you bring something new into your life, you remove something old. A new pair of shoes means one pair leaves. A new subscription means one is cancelled. A new commitment means one is finished or declined.

## Why it works

**It adds a pause.** The question "what would I give up?" makes you notice whether you really want the new thing.

**It keeps totals steady.** Your belongings, costs and obligations stop growing by default.

**It sharpens priorities.** If nothing you own feels worth removing, the new thing probably isn't needed.

## Where to apply it

- **Clothes and household items.** Donate or sell what leaves.
- **Subscriptions and memberships.** Keep a short list and stick to a number.
- **Apps and digital tools.** Delete one when you install one.
- **Commitments.** Swap, don't stack.

## Add a waiting period

For anything above a certain amount, wait 48 hours before buying. Many urges fade on their own.

## Turn the outgoing item into value

Sell or donate it. Put any proceeds into a small goal fund. It makes decluttering feel productive.

## What to watch for

Don't use the rule to excuse unnecessary buying: "I'm giving something away, so it's fine." The first question is still whether you need the new thing at all.

## Try it for a month

Choose one category, such as subscriptions, and apply the rule. Notice how your decisions change.

Less quietly accumulating means more quietly available, for time, money and attention.
