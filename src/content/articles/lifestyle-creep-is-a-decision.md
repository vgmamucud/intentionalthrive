Nobody decides to spend all of a raise. It just happens.

A slightly better phone. A nicer place to eat on Fridays. A subscription here, an upgrade there. A year later, your income is higher and your savings haven't moved.

That's lifestyle creep. It isn't a character flaw. It's what happens when spending is left on autopilot.

## Why it's so easy

Each upgrade is small, reasonable and justified. "I work hard." "It's only a little more." And it is. The problem is that small increases become new baselines, and baselines are hard to lower.

## The raise rule

Before a raise or bonus arrives, decide how it splits. A simple version:

- **Half to your future:** savings, protection, investments.
- **Half to your life:** the upgrades you've actually been wanting.

You still get to enjoy it. But your future gets a share by default, not by leftovers.

Choose your own proportions. The point is to choose them **before** the money lands.

## Audit the baseline

Once a year, look at three months of statements and ask of each recurring expense:

1. **Would I sign up for this again today?**
2. **Does it still earn its place?**
3. **What did it replace when I added it?**

Cancel what fails. You'll often find an old decision still charging you.

## Spend on purpose

Creep isn't spending, it's *unconscious* spending. Money spent deliberately on something you value isn't creep. A trip you planned and loved is not the same as ten small purchases you can't remember.

## A useful question

When you're about to upgrade something, ask: *am I paying for the thing, or for what it signals?* If it's for the thing, enjoy it. If it's for the signal, wait a week.

Your income is a tool. Decide what it builds.
