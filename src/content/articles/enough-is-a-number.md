Ask a dozen people how much money would be enough, and most will say "more than I have now."

That answer never moves. Whatever you earn, it stays slightly ahead of you, like a horizon.

## Why "enough" keeps escaping

When enough is a feeling, it follows your circle. A raise resets your comparison point. A friend's new car resets it again. You aren't failing at contentment. You're measuring with a ruler that stretches.

When enough is a **number**, it can be reached.

## How to find your number

Not a dream number. A working one.

**1. Your essentials.** What does your household need each month to live safely? Housing, food, utilities, transport, school, health, the support you give family.

**2. Your comfort layer.** What would make life genuinely good, without excess? Rest, small treats, time with people you love.

**3. Your protection layer.** Premiums, emergency savings, the cost of keeping others safe.

**4. Your future layer.** What you need to set aside so work becomes optional someday.

Add them up. That's your monthly "enough." Multiply by twelve and you have a yearly number you can plan around.

## What changes once you have it

- **Raises become choices,** not automatic lifestyle upgrades.
- **Saving has a target** instead of a vague hope.
- **You notice when you're spending beyond it,** and can ask why.
- **You can relax at the number,** instead of treating every month as a race.

## The honest caveat

Your number will change as life does: a child, a parent who needs care, a move. That's fine. A number you revisit is still better than a feeling you never name.

## Try it this week

Write your four layers on a single page. Put a peso figure next to each. Then circle the one that surprised you.

Enough isn't about wanting less. It's about knowing what you're building toward, so you can recognise when you've arrived.
