Ask for a raise and feel your stomach drop. Charge fairly for your work and apologise in the same sentence. Say you want to build wealth and add "but not too much, of course."

If that sounds familiar, you may have inherited a quiet rule: wanting money makes you greedy.

## Where it comes from

Humility is a real value, and so is generosity. But somewhere they got tangled up with a belief that ambition about money is vulgar, that "good people" are content with enough and don't talk about it.

The rule usually has a kind intention behind it: don't be a show-off, don't put money above people. It just doesn't distinguish between greed and ordinary, healthy wanting.

## Greed and wanting aren't the same

**Greed** takes more than is fair and doesn't care who pays.

**Wanting** is wishing for security, options and the ability to help the people you love. It's not a flaw. It's a plan.

## What the fear tends to cost

- Underpricing your time, and burning out to make up for it.
- Staying quiet in negotiations.
- Avoiding growth because it feels like boasting.
- Helping everyone else while your own plan waits.

## Reframe the want

Try finishing this: **"I want more money so that..."**

So I can protect my parents. So my child has options. So I can say no to a job that drains me. So I can give without worrying.

Once the money is attached to people and purposes, it stops sounding like greed and starts sounding like care.

## One small act

This week, do one thing the fear usually blocks: send the invoice at the fair rate, ask the question about pay, or write down the number you actually want to build toward. Notice that the world carries on.

You can be humble and ambitious. Most of the people worth admiring are both.
