"Hindi pa kaya ngayon." Not yet. Not now.

It's a phrase worth taking seriously, because sometimes it's true. Money really is tight. Priorities really do compete.

But it's also one of the most common sentences people use to postpone protection, saving and planning, and its cost grows quietly with every month we repeat it.

## When "not yet" is honest

It's honest when you've looked at your numbers, found there's genuinely no room, and chosen a date to look again. That's a decision.

## When it's a habit

It's a habit when it's said without looking at the numbers at all, when it has no date attached, or when the same answer has stood for years while income has grown.

A simple test: **when would it be "kaya na"?** If you can't name a number or a date, the sentence isn't describing your finances. It's describing a feeling.

## What waiting costs

Premiums rise with age. Health can change. The savings you didn't start compound for nobody. And the problem you were waiting to feel ready for usually arrives anyway.

None of this is a reason to panic. It's a reason to replace "not yet" with something specific.

## Three replacements

1. **"Not at this amount, but at this one."** A smaller start beats none. Even ₱500 a month begins the habit.
2. **"Not now, but on this date."** Put the review in your calendar.
3. **"Not alone."** Ask someone who can show you the options without pressure.

## Try it this week

Pick one thing you've been postponing. Write: *"It will become possible when ___, and I'll check again on ___."* Then look at your numbers once, honestly.

You may find there's more room than you thought, or you may find a clear reason to wait. Either way, you'll have moved from feeling to deciding.
