A fund fact sheet is a one-page summary of an investment fund. It looks technical, but five items tell you most of what you need.

## 1. The objective

What is the fund trying to do? Grow capital, earn income, or preserve value? An aggressive growth fund and a conservative income fund behave very differently.

## 2. The risk rating

Funds are usually labelled with a risk level. Match it honestly to your timeline and your stomach. If a 20% drop would make you sell in panic, a high-risk fund is the wrong fit regardless of past returns.

## 3. What it holds

Look at the allocation: how much is in stocks, bonds or cash, and the largest holdings. This tells you what drives the fund's ups and downs.

## 4. The costs

Find the management fee and any other charges. Small percentages matter over many years. Ask what's deducted and how often.

## 5. The track record, carefully

Look at performance over several years, not last quarter, and compare with the fund's own benchmark, if shown. Remember: past performance is not a promise.

## What to treat with care

- **A single best year** displayed in large type.
- **Short-term charts** that make a fund look steadier or hotter than it is.
- **Jargon** you can't translate. If you can't explain it to a friend, ask.

## A quick checklist

- Does the objective match my goal?
- Is the risk level one I can hold through a downturn?
- Do I understand what it invests in?
- Do I know the costs?
- Is my time horizon long enough?

## Practice

Download one fact sheet for a fund you already own, or are considering, and answer the five questions on paper. If you can't, you've found what to ask your advisor.

Good investing is mostly a matter of knowing what you own and why.
