Want to know what you really value? Don't read your goals. Read your calendar.

Where your hours go is the plainest evidence of your priorities, and for many of us it tells a different story from the one we'd like.

## Run a one-week audit

Look back at the last two weeks. Roughly how many hours went to:

- Work and commuting
- Family and relationships
- Health and rest
- Learning and growth
- Faith, community or service
- Things you did not choose

You don't need precision. A rough picture is enough.

## Compare with your values

Write your top five values, such as family, health, faith, learning, security. Next to each, write the hours it received. Where's the gap?

A gap isn't a failure. It's information.

## Put values on the calendar first

Instead of fitting your values into whatever's left, schedule them as fixed blocks:

- **Family time,** named and recurring.
- **A weekly rest block** that isn't available for meetings.
- **Money time,** twenty minutes a week for your finances.
- **A learning slot.**

Then build the rest around them.

## Protect the blocks

Treat them like appointments with someone important. If one moves, reschedule it, don't delete it.

## Review quarterly

Seasons change. Reassess every few months whether the blocks still reflect what you want.

## Start small

Choose one value that is currently underfed. Add a single recurring block for it this week.

Your calendar is a vote. Make sure it's going where you want.
