A common sequence in personal finance goes: invest as early as possible, worry about everything else later.

It sounds efficient. It can also be the reason a family loses years of progress in a single bad month.

## Why the order matters

Investments grow over decades, but they're also reachable. If an emergency hits and your protection is thin, the first thing you'll do is sell or withdraw them, often at a bad time, and often with penalties.

Protection exists so growth is allowed to finish.

## A sensible order

1. **Starter cash buffer.** Enough for ordinary shocks.
2. **Health cover.** So one admission doesn't consume your savings.
3. **Income and life protection.** In proportion to who depends on you.
4. **Debt that costs more than you could earn.** High-interest balances come before most investments.
5. **Long-term investing.** With a plan, regular amounts, and time.
6. **Expansion:** extra properties, business, education funds.

The sequence isn't rigid. Life interleaves the steps. But skipping the early ones is where plans break.

## Common mistakes

- **Buying an investment product mainly for its insurance, or an insurance product mainly for its returns.** Know what each peso is for.
- **Over-insuring and under-saving,** or the reverse.
- **Starting big, stopping soon.** Small, consistent amounts outlast ambitious ones.
- **Ignoring fees.** They matter over decades.

## How to check your own order

Draw four boxes: cash, protection, debt, investments. Write what you have in each. If the investment box is full and the first two are empty, the foundation needs attention.

Growth is the exciting part. Protection is what allows it to happen.
