Somewhere along the way, many of us started reading our bank balance as a report card.

A low number feels like a failing grade. A high one feels like permission to relax. Neither says anything about whether you're a good person, a capable one, or a worthy one.

## Where the link comes from

We're surrounded by signals that tie worth to wealth: the house, the car, the trip, the title. And in families where money was scarce, being "able to provide" often became the main way love was shown. It's no surprise that the number started to feel like the person.

## What it costs to merge them

**Hiding.** If your finances feel like a verdict, you avoid looking at them, and avoidance is the most expensive habit there is.

**Overspending to prove something.** Buying the appearance of success so nobody sees the gap.

**Shame that blocks help.** You postpone the conversation that would fix things because the conversation feels like a confession.

## Separate the two on paper

Try this small exercise.

1. Write your net worth on one line.
2. Under it, write three things you're good at or proud of that have no peso value.
3. Read both lines. Notice that they can both be true.

Your net worth is a snapshot of what you've accumulated so far, shaped by timing, family, income, luck and choices. Useful information. Not a ranking.

## Use the number, don't wear it

A low number tells you where to start. A high one tells you what you have to protect. In either case, it's data for decisions, not a measure of you.

Treat it like a thermometer. You wouldn't feel ashamed of a temperature. You'd just decide what to do next.
