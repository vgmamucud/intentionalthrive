Most people choose a beneficiary once, at the signing table, and never look at it again. Meanwhile life moves: marriages, children, deaths, separations, new responsibilities.

A beneficiary form that no longer matches your life is one of the quietest and most painful paperwork problems.

## What the beneficiary does

Your named beneficiary is the person or people who receive the death benefit. It generally goes to them directly, according to your policy and the applicable rules, which is why the name on the form matters so much.

## When to review it

Look at it after any of these:

- Marriage or separation.
- Birth or adoption of a child.
- The death of a named beneficiary.
- A change in who depends on you.
- Any time you buy or change a policy.

## Common problems

**An outdated name.** A former partner still listed, or a parent who has passed.

**A minor named directly.** Where beneficiaries are children, there are special considerations about who can receive and manage the money on their behalf. Ask your insurer how this is handled for your policy.

**Vague wording.** "My family" isn't a beneficiary designation. Names and relationships are.

**Nobody knows.** The person named has never been told a policy exists.

## Revocable or irrevocable

Some designations can be changed freely; others can't without the beneficiary's consent. Check which applies to yours before assuming you can update it.

## How to update

1. Get the beneficiary form from your insurer or advisor.
2. Fill it with full names, relationships and shares.
3. Sign and submit it, and keep a copy.
4. Ask for written confirmation that it's been recorded.

## Do this week

Find every policy you own and write down who is named on each. If any name is out of date, start the change. And tell the people named where the documents are.

It's an hour of work that spares your family a very long year.
