You open your phone for five minutes. In that time you've seen a new house, a holiday, a car and a launch party.

None of it was your plan. But now your own life looks slightly smaller than it did this morning.

## What comparison actually does

It changes your baseline. What felt like enough yesterday feels behind today. And behind creates urgency, which creates spending.

I call it the comparison tax: the extra money you spend, not because you want something, but because you feel you should have it by now.

## Where it shows up

- Upgrading things that still work.
- Saying yes to events you can't comfortably afford.
- Buying "investments" because everyone seems to be in on something.
- Avoiding your own plan because it looks modest next to someone's highlight reel.

## What you're not seeing

Highlight reels hide the loans, the leverage, the family help and the bad months. Often the person you envy is financing the picture. Often they're anxious too.

You're comparing your full ledger to someone else's cover photo.

## Ways to reduce the tax

**1. Curate your feed.** Mute accounts that trigger spending urges. It's your attention; you're allowed to manage it.

**2. Delay purchases by 48 hours** if they appear after scrolling. Most urges fade.

**3. Write your own scoreboard.** Savings rate, protection in place, debts reduced. Metrics nobody else sees but you.

**4. Celebrate quietly.** Not every milestone needs an audience. A fully funded emergency account is a good reason to smile.

**5. Remember the timeline.** Everyone is at a different stage. Your pace is yours.

## A better question

Instead of "why don't I have that?", ask "what do I actually want my money to do?" The second question has an answer. The first one never ends.

Spend with your plan in your hand, not someone else's feed in your head.
