Most of us do a review once a year, in the last week of December, tired and full of leftover food. It's too late to change much and too far from the beginning to remember what we intended.

Four short reviews do more than one long one.

## The idea

Every three months, take ninety minutes. Alone, with a notebook, somewhere quiet. Review what happened and set the next quarter.

Pick dates now: the end of March, June, September and December.

## The five sections

**1. What happened? (15 minutes)**
Scroll your calendar and photos. Write the highlights, the disappointments and what surprised you.

**2. Money check (20 minutes)**
- Net worth, roughly.
- Savings added.
- Debts changed.
- Protection: anything lapsing or outdated?
- Anything you spent that you'd rather not have?

**3. Energy and health (15 minutes)**
How's your sleep, movement, stress? What is draining you? What gave you energy?

**4. Relationships (10 minutes)**
Who deserves more of your time? Is there a conversation you've been avoiding?

**5. Next quarter (30 minutes)**
Choose three goals. One for money, one for life, one for you. Write the first step and the date for each.

## Rules

- **No guilt.** This is a review, not a trial.
- **Write it down.** Memory edits the story.
- **Keep it short.** If it takes over an afternoon, you'll skip it.
- **Share one thing** with someone you trust, so it's real.

## When life interrupts

If a quarter was chaotic, do a shorter version. A tired review still beats none.

## What it gives you

Direction, and also early warnings. You notice a lapsed premium in March, not in December. You catch burnout before it takes over. You course-correct while it's cheap.

Book your first one now. Put it in the calendar before you finish reading this.
