"That's expensive."

We say it so quickly that it feels like a measurement. Like we've weighed the price against the world and found it heavy.

But expensive isn't a property of the thing. It's a relationship between a price and what you're comparing it to.

## What the word usually means

When you say "expensive," one of these is almost always underneath:

- **"I can't afford it."** A real limit. Fine. Say that.
- **"I don't value it enough."** Also fine, and honest.
- **"I'm afraid to commit."** Often the truth for protection, learning or health.
- **"I haven't compared it to the alternative."** The most common one.

## Compare against the cost of not having it

A premium looks expensive next to this month's budget. It looks very different next to a hospital stay, a missed income month or a family left scrambling.

A course looks expensive next to a night out. It looks different next to the income it could open.

Cheap and expensive only make sense against something. Choose the something on purpose.

## Try replacing the word

Next time you say "that's expensive," finish this sentence instead: **"I'm choosing not to because..."**

Because I need that money for something I've already decided matters more. Because I'm not sure it works. Because I'm afraid of being wrong.

Sometimes the honest sentence ends the discussion. Sometimes it opens a better one.

## A better habit

For any purchase over a threshold you set, such as ₱3,000, wait 48 hours and write down what you're comparing it to. If you can name the alternative, you can decide. If you can't, you weren't judging the price. You were reacting.

Expensive can still be the right answer. It should just be an answer you chose.
