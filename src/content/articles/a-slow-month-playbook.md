If your income moves with sales, clients or commissions, you know the feeling. A month arrives that's quieter than planned, and everything feels louder: the bills, the doubt, the comparison.

I'd rather you meet that month with a plan than with panic.

## Step 1: Look, don't flinch

Open your numbers. How much is coming, and what's due? Work out the real gap. Many slow months turn out less frightening once they have a figure.

## Step 2: Protect the non-negotiables

List what must be paid first: housing, utilities, food, protection premiums, minimum debt payments. Keep your premiums alive. A lapse in a thin month becomes a larger problem later.

## Step 3: Use the buffer, on purpose

This is what a cash buffer is for. Take from it deliberately, write down the amount, and plan how to refill it when income recovers. See [Build Your Cash Buffer in Three Tiers](/blog/build-your-cash-buffer-in-three-tiers).

## Step 4: Pause the optional

Choose what stops for now: subscriptions, upgrades, extra outings. Name it as temporary so it doesn't feel like loss.

## Step 5: Do the work that compounds

Slow months are often the right time for things you postponed: following up with people, learning a skill, tidying your systems, reaching out to those you haven't spoken to. Spend the free time on activity that pays later.

## Step 6: Talk to someone

If you can't cover something, contact the lender or biller early. Most people are more willing to arrange a plan before a payment is missed than after.

## Step 7: Don't change who you are

Resist making rushed decisions: a risky loan, an aggressive sale, a promise you can't keep. A slow month is a temporary condition, not a verdict.

## After it passes

Write down what you learned. What would have helped? A larger buffer, a smaller fixed cost, an earlier follow-up? Adjust the plan.

Steady isn't the absence of slow months. It's knowing what to do when they come.
