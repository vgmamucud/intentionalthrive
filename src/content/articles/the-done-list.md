Most of us end the day looking at what we didn't finish. The to-do list stays long. The feeling of having fallen behind stays with us.

A done list changes the evidence.

## What it is

At the end of the day, you write down what you did. Not what you planned. What actually happened. It takes two minutes.

## Why it works

**It corrects a skewed view.** We remember the unfinished and forget the finished. Writing it down restores the balance.

**It reveals where your time went.** Often the day was spent on useful things that were never on the plan.

**It builds confidence.** Over weeks, you accumulate proof that you move things forward.

**It improves planning.** Seeing what you actually completed helps you make more realistic lists.

## How to do it

1. Keep a notebook or note on your phone.
2. Before you close the day, write five to ten things you did. Include small things: calls, conversations, decisions, errors fixed, the meal you cooked, the time you spent with your child.
3. Circle one thing you're glad about.

## Include the invisible work

A lot of valuable work doesn't make it onto to-do lists: listening, caring for others, tidying a problem before it grew. Write those down too.

## Add one money line

Note any money action, however small: paid a bill, moved savings, reviewed a statement. It trains you to see your financial life as something you tend, not something that happens to you.

## A weekly look back

On Sunday, read the week's lists. It's a quiet and surprisingly motivating way to see your own progress.

Tonight, write three things you did today. That's the whole beginning.
