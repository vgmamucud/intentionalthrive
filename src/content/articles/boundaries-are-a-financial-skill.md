We usually file boundaries under emotional health. They belong under finance too.

Every unplanned yes has a price: an hour you didn't spend resting, a favour that became a loan, a weekend that disappeared into someone else's emergency.

## Where boundaries leak money

- **Work:** unpaid extra scope, constant availability, discounting because it's awkward to ask for more.
- **Family:** repeated financial requests with no limit.
- **Social:** events you attend out of obligation, with the cost of attending.
- **Business:** clients who take more time than they pay for.

## Three kinds of no

**The warm no:** "Thank you for thinking of me. I can't this time."

**The not-yet:** "I can't do it now. I could look at it next month."

**The counter-offer:** "I can't do all of that. I can do this part."

You don't need a long explanation. Over-explaining invites negotiation.

## Prepare in advance

The hard time to set a boundary is mid-conversation. So decide ahead:

1. **How much time or money can I give without strain?**
2. **What are my non-negotiables?** Sleep, family time, a day off.
3. **What would I say if asked?** Write a sentence for each recurring situation.

## When pushback arrives

Expect it, especially from people who benefit from your yes. Stay calm, repeat your answer once, and don't escalate. Discomfort is not proof you were wrong.

## Boundaries and protection

If your plan is that you'll absorb every crisis, you'll need a bigger safety net than anyone has. Boundaries and coverage work together: coverage handles the big shocks, boundaries handle the daily leakage.

## Practise small

This week, say no to one small thing you'd usually accept. Notice how it feels. Then notice that nothing collapsed.

Your time and money are the raw materials of your plan. Protecting them is part of the plan.
