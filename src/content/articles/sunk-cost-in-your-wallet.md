Sunk cost is a simple idea with a stubborn grip: we keep investing in something because of what we've already put in, not because of what it will give us from here.

It's behind the gym membership you haven't used in months, the course you never finished, the old plan you're still paying for, and the business you keep funding because quitting would "waste" everything so far.

## Why it holds on

Letting go feels like admitting a loss. As long as you keep paying, the loss stays theoretical. Stopping makes it real.

But the money is gone either way. The only decision left is about the future.

## The one question

For any ongoing cost, ask: **"If I weren't already paying for this, would I start today?"**

If the answer is yes, keep it with a clear conscience. If it's no, the past spending isn't a reason to continue.

## Where to look

- Subscriptions and memberships you don't use.
- A product you bought years ago that no longer fits your life.
- A project or side business that drains time and money without a plan.
- Gifts, clothes or equipment you keep "in case."

## A careful note about insurance

Not every old commitment should be dropped. A policy that's no longer right for you might still have value, penalties for leaving early, or health history that makes replacing it costly. Before cancelling anything, ask for the surrender value, the cost of replacement and what you'd lose. Then decide with numbers, not feelings.

## Do a one-hour audit

Open three months of statements. Mark each recurring charge with one of three labels: **keep, review, stop.** Stop the obvious ones today. Put the "review" ones in your calendar with a date.

You don't owe your past spending your loyalty. You owe your future yourself.
