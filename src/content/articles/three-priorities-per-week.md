A to-do list with thirty items isn't a plan. It's a worry written down.

If your weeks feel busy but empty, the problem might be that everything has equal weight.

## The three-priority rule

Each week, choose three things that, if done, would make the week a success. Not thirty. Three.

Everything else is either maintenance, or it can wait.

## How to pick them

Ask:

1. **What moves something that matters forward?** A project, a relationship, your health, your finances.
2. **What has a real deadline?**
3. **What will I be glad I did on Friday?**

Aim for a mix across your life, not three work items. For example: one for work, one for home or family, one for you.

## Protect them in the calendar

A priority without a time slot is a wish. Block the time for each. Treat those slots like appointments with someone important, because they are.

## Handling the rest

Your other tasks still exist. Put them in a separate list and tackle them in the gaps. If something from that list keeps showing up for weeks, either schedule it as a priority or delete it.

## A Sunday setup

Spend ten minutes on Sunday evening:

- Look at the week's calendar.
- Choose the three priorities.
- Block time.
- Pick one thing to say no to.

Pair it with the [Sunday reset](/blog/sunday-reset) if you already do one.

## Review on Friday

Don't judge yourself. Just notice: did I do the three? If not, why? Was the plan unrealistic, or did something more important appear? Both are useful.

## Why this helps your money

Financial tasks are the type that get postponed: the review, the call, the form. Making them a weekly priority, even once a month, is how they get done.

Three things. Then rest.
