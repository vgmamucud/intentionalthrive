Turning down something bad is easy. The difficulty is saying no to opportunities that are good, flattering or kind, but that don't belong in your season.

Every yes spends something: time, energy, attention or money. A calendar full of good things can leave no room for the important ones.

## The hidden cost of yes

Each commitment carries more than its obvious time. There's preparation, travel, recovery and the mental space it takes up even when you're not doing it.

Before saying yes, count the whole cost, not just the hour on the invitation.

## Four questions

1. **Does this move something I've already decided matters?** If your top priorities are clear, this question is quick.
2. **What will I stop doing to make room?** If the answer is "sleep" or "family time," think again.
3. **Would I say yes if it were next week?** Distance tempts us to over-commit. Imagine it happening soon.
4. **Is this a yes from desire or from guilt?** Both are human. Only one is a good reason.

## Scripts that keep the warmth

- *"Thank you for thinking of me. I can't take this on right now."*
- *"I'd love to, but I'm protecting this season for other commitments."*
- *"Not this time, but please keep me in mind."*

You don't have to explain at length. Brief and kind is usually enough.

## A sustainable "yes" budget

Decide how many ongoing commitments you can hold at once, such as three big ones, and treat that number as a ceiling. A new yes means an old one ends.

## The payoff

Saying no to good things is how you make room for great ones. It protects your health, your family and the work only you can do.

Pick one commitment you've been holding out of guilt, and decide this week whether it still earns its place.
