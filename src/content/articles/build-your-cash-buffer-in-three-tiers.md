"Save six months of expenses" is correct and also paralysing. If you have nothing set aside, six months feels like a mountain with no path.

So break it into tiers. Each one is useful on its own.

## Tier 1: The one-month floor

**Goal: one month of essential expenses.**

This stops small shocks (a repair, a medical co-pay, a late paycheck) from becoming loans. Keep it somewhere easy to reach.

Don't aim higher yet. Get to the floor first and feel the difference.

## Tier 2: The three-month cushion

**Goal: three months of essentials.**

This covers a gap between jobs, a slower business quarter, or a longer recovery. It's the level at which you start to make decisions from choice rather than pressure.

## Tier 3: The full buffer

**Goal: six months or more, depending on your situation.**

Aim higher if you're self-employed, commission-based, the sole earner, or supporting multiple dependents. Lower is acceptable if you have steady income, shared household income and strong cover.

## Where to keep it

- **Separate from daily spending,** so it isn't absorbed by the month.
- **Accessible within a day or two,** not locked for years.
- **Safe,** not invested in anything that can drop when you need it.

## How to build it

1. **Calculate your essentials.** Housing, food, utilities, transport, school, minimum debt payments, insurance, family support.
2. **Automate a fixed amount** on payday, before you see it.
3. **Direct windfalls:** a share of bonuses and the 13th month goes here until the tier is full.
4. **Name the account,** so you remember what it's for.
5. **Refill after use.** Using it is its purpose. Rebuilding it is the habit.

## Where protection fits

A fund covers small and medium shocks. Large ones, such as long illness or loss of income, need insurance. The two work together. If you want the order, read [what life insurance actually is](/blog/what-life-insurance-actually-is).

Start with tier one this month. Not perfect, just started.
