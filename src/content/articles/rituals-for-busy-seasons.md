There are seasons that overwhelm any plan: a new baby, a launch, a hospital stay, a year-end rush, a family crisis. Your usual routines don't survive them.

That doesn't mean you drop everything. It means you shrink your commitments to the essentials, and call them rituals.

## Routines vs rituals

A **routine** is a longer sequence that needs time and energy. A **ritual** is a small, repeatable act that anchors you, even if it takes two minutes.

In busy seasons, rituals keep you connected to what matters.

## Choose three

Pick one for each of three areas:

**Body.** A glass of water on waking. A five-minute walk. A stretch before bed.

**Mind.** One written line each night. Three slow breaths before opening your phone. A short prayer.

**Money.** A two-minute check of your balance on Sunday. Confirming that premiums and bills are on autopay.

## Make them tiny

If you can't do it on your worst day, it's too big. Shrink it until it takes less than two minutes and requires no preparation.

## Attach them to something that already happens

After brushing teeth. After the kettle boils. After you put the child to bed. A ritual stuck to an existing habit survives.

## Drop what you must

Decide in advance what pauses: the long workout, the big planning session, the weekly review. Naming it prevents guilt.

## Return gently

When the season eases, rebuild one routine at a time rather than all at once.

## A reminder

You're not failing when you shrink. You're adapting. Keeping a little of the structure is what lets you return to the rest.

Pick your three rituals tonight, before the next busy stretch arrives.
