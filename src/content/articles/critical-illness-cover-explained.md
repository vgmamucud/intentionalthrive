Critical illness cover is often sold with fear. It doesn't need to be. It's a practical tool for a specific problem: what happens to your life, not just your hospital bill, if you're diagnosed with a serious condition.

## What it does

If you're diagnosed with a condition listed in your contract, and you meet its definition, a critical illness benefit pays a **lump sum** to you. It doesn't depend on the size of your hospital bill, and you decide how to use it.

## The problem it solves

Hospital cover pays for treatment. It doesn't replace your salary while you recover, pay the helper, cover travel to a specialist or keep a business running. Serious illness creates costs and income gaps that bills don't show.

## What to read in your policy

**The list.** Which conditions are covered? The list is the product.

**The definitions.** Each condition has precise criteria. Two people with the same diagnosis may be treated differently depending on severity.

**Early-stage benefits.** Some plans pay a smaller amount for early stages. Know whether yours does.

**Waiting and survival periods.** Many contracts have a period after issue before a diagnosis counts, and some require you to survive a number of days after diagnosis.

**What happens next.** Does the rider end after a claim? Does the main benefit reduce?

## How much is enough?

Take your monthly household spending. Multiply by the number of months you'd realistically need to step back from work or recover. Compare it with the lump sum on your policy.

## A word on timing

Cover is easier to get, and usually cheaper, when you're healthy. Waiting until you're worried is when options narrow.

## Do this week

Find your policy's list of covered conditions. Count them. Write down the lump sum and the waiting period. Then ask: if I were diagnosed next month, would this amount buy me the time I'd need?
