People often ask which type of life insurance is "best." That's like asking whether a hammer or a screwdriver is better. It depends on what you're trying to fix.

## Term insurance: protection for a set period

You pay a lower premium for a defined number of years. If you die within the term, your family receives the benefit. If you don't, it ends and there's no payout.

**Good for:** a large amount of protection during high-responsibility years, such as young children or active loans.

**Watch for:** what happens at the end. Ask about renewability and conversion options.

## Whole life: protection that lasts, with guaranteed features

Permanent coverage with premiums that are generally higher, and typically a guaranteed cash value that builds over time, depending on the product.

**Good for:** needs that never end, like final expenses or leaving something behind, for people who prefer guarantees and will keep paying.

**Watch for:** early surrender, when the cash value is usually lowest.

## VUL: protection plus market-linked investment

A death benefit combined with funds that rise and fall with the market. The investment value isn't guaranteed.

**Good for:** people with a long horizon, a tolerance for fluctuation and the discipline to keep paying through tight months. See [The VUL Truth](/blog/vul-truth).

**Watch for:** charges, lapse risk and expecting it to behave like a savings account.

## Three questions to choose

1. **How long does the need last?** A time-limited need points to term. A lifelong one points to permanent.
2. **What can I pay in my worst month?** Choose the plan you can keep.
3. **Do I want guarantees or growth potential?** And am I comfortable with the trade-offs of each?

## A common, sensible mix

Many people pair a larger term policy for the high-responsibility years with a smaller permanent plan for the lifelong needs.

Whatever you choose, get the illustration, read the guaranteed columns, and take it home before deciding.
