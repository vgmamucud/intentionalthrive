Debt is one of the most common financial situations there is. It's also one of the most hidden.

People who would talk openly about a health problem will go quiet about a card balance. The shame can feel heavier than the money.

But shame doesn't pay debt down. A plan does, and a plan can only start when you can look at the numbers.

## Why we hide it

In many families, being in debt is read as a character flaw, not a circumstance. So we cover it, borrow quietly, and avoid opening the statement. The avoidance makes it worse: interest keeps running whether you look or not.

## Debt is information

Debt tells you something happened: an emergency, a gap between income and expenses, a decision that didn't work out, a family need. It's worth understanding without judging.

## Step 1: List it all

One page. For each debt: who it's owed to, the balance, the interest rate or cost, and the minimum payment. Include informal loans.

## Step 2: Find the expensive ones

Rank them by cost. High-interest balances usually come first, because they grow fastest.

## Step 3: Pick a method

- **Avalanche:** extra payments go to the highest-cost debt. It saves the most money.
- **Snowball:** extra payments go to the smallest balance. It gives quicker wins and helps some people stay motivated.

Either works if you keep going. The best one is the one you'll stick with.

## Step 4: Stop adding to it

Look at what created the debt. If it's recurring, such as a monthly shortfall or an uncovered emergency, the plan needs a small buffer as well, or the cycle repeats.

## Step 5: Tell one person

Choose someone safe. Shame shrinks when it's spoken. If your debt is large or overwhelming, a licensed financial counselor or your lender may be able to discuss options.

## What changes

Nothing about your worth. Only the next step.

You're not behind. You're at the start of a plan.
