If financial knowledge were enough, most of us would be wealthy. We know we should save, review our coverage, keep a buffer and avoid impulse buying.

The gap isn't information. It's the distance between knowing and doing.

## Why the gap exists

**Effort now, reward later.** The brain discounts distant rewards.

**Friction.** Each step needs a form, a call or a decision.

**Emotion.** Money tasks come with worry, shame or boredom attached.

**Vagueness.** "Get my finances in order" isn't an action. It's a mood.

## Narrow it with specificity

Turn each intention into a concrete step with a time:

- Not "save more," but "transfer ₱2,000 on the 16th."
- Not "review my insurance," but "find my policies on Saturday at 10."
- Not "invest," but "read one fact sheet on Sunday."

## Reduce the friction

Pre-fill forms. Set autopay. Keep the policy folder in one place. Make the first step so small that it's easier to do than to avoid.

## Add a witness

Tell someone what you'll do, and when. A friend, partner or mentor. A simple "I'll message you when it's done" doubles follow-through for many people.

## Start before you feel ready

Waiting for motivation is a trap. Do the two-minute version: open the app, look at the number, write the next step. Momentum often follows action.

## Forgive the lapses

You will skip things. Return without drama. The goal isn't an unbroken streak, it's a pattern that continues.

## One thing

Choose a task you've known about for months. Write: *"I will ___ on ___ at ___."* Put it in your calendar, tell someone, and do the first two minutes today.

Knowing is the start. Doing is where it changes.
