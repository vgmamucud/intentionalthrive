Open your inbox and search for "statement." How many accounts appear that you'd forgotten?

Digital clutter isn't just untidy. It hides money, and it hides problems.

## What clutter costs

- **Subscriptions** that continue charging after you've stopped using them.
- **Small balances** in forgotten accounts.
- **Missed notices** about policies, renewals and fee changes.
- **Time lost** searching for a document when you need it urgently.
- **Family stress** if something happens to you and no one can find anything.

## A one-hour clean-up

**1. List your accounts (15 minutes).** Banks, e-wallets, investment platforms, insurers, loans, subscriptions. Write the name only.

**2. Close what you don't use (15 minutes).** Check balances first. Consolidate where possible.

**3. Cancel unused subscriptions (10 minutes).** Review one month of statements.

**4. Set statements to a single email (10 minutes).** Create a folder or label for them.

**5. Create one master document (10 minutes).** For each account: provider, what it's for, where the details are kept. No passwords in it. Store passwords in a proper password manager.

## Do the same for policies

Gather policy numbers, insurers, beneficiaries, and the contact for claims. Keep a copy somewhere trusted people can reach.

If you wondered what's missing, [the Sunday reset](/blog/sunday-reset) is a helpful companion habit.

## Tell someone

A tidy system no one else knows about is only half useful. Tell one person where the master document is and how to open it in an emergency.

## Keep it light

Review twice a year. New account? Add a line. Closed one? Remove it.

Clarity is a form of safety. When your information is organised, everything else gets easier to manage.
