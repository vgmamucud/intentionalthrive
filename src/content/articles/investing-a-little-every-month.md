Many people postpone investing because they feel they need a big amount or the right moment. Regular monthly investing removes both problems.

## The idea

Instead of investing a lump sum, you invest a fixed amount on a fixed date every month. When prices are high, your amount buys fewer units. When prices are low, it buys more. Over time you pay an average price, and you stop trying to guess the best day.

This approach is often called peso-cost averaging or dollar-cost averaging.

## Why it suits real life

**It fits your cash flow.** You invest from your salary, not from savings you don't have yet.

**It builds a habit.** The behaviour matters more than the starting amount.

**It reduces regret.** You don't have to wonder whether you bought at the top.

## What it doesn't do

It doesn't guarantee a profit, and it doesn't protect you from losses in a falling market. It's a way of entering the market gradually, not a way of avoiding risk.

## How to start

1. **Do the foundations first:** starter emergency fund and basic protection.
2. **Pick an amount you can keep** even in a tight month.
3. **Choose a fund that matches your timeline and risk comfort**, and read its fact sheet.
4. **Automate it,** the day after payday.
5. **Review once or twice a year,** not every week.

## Common mistakes

- Starting with an amount that's too ambitious and stopping within months.
- Checking daily and reacting to every dip.
- Stopping when markets fall, which is when it works hardest.

## A small start is still a start

Even a modest monthly amount, kept up for many years, can grow into something meaningful. The most important number isn't the size. It's how long you keep going.

Treat it like a bill you owe your future self.
