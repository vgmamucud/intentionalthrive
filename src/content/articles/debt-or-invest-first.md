It's one of the most common money questions: should I pay off what I owe, or start investing? Both are good. The order depends on what the debt costs.

## Compare the costs

Every peso you use to pay down debt earns a guaranteed return equal to the interest you stop paying. So the question becomes: could my investments reasonably earn more than my debt costs, after risk?

For high-interest debt like credit cards, the answer is almost always no. Paying it down first is the stronger move.

## A sensible order

1. **Starter emergency fund,** so a surprise doesn't push you back into debt.
2. **Basic protection,** so one event doesn't undo everything.
3. **Minimum payments on all debts,** always.
4. **Extra payments to the highest-cost debt.**
5. **Then, or alongside,** begin investing a modest amount.

## When both can happen together

If your debt is low-cost, such as a long-term housing loan at a modest rate, there's often room to invest while making regular payments. You're balancing certainty against growth, and many people choose to do some of each.

## Peace of mind counts too

A guaranteed result matters. Paying off a card removes a source of stress that numbers don't capture. If a balance keeps you awake, paying it down is a legitimate choice, even when a spreadsheet says otherwise.

## What to avoid

- Investing while carrying high-interest balances you could clear.
- Draining your emergency fund to pay debt early, then borrowing again.
- Waiting to be debt-free before saving anything at all.

## Do this week

List your debts with their interest costs. Circle the highest. Decide an extra amount you can add this month. If you're not sure how to split between debt and investing, bring the list to someone who can look at the numbers with you.
