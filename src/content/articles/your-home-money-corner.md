When a bill arrives, a statement is needed or a policy must be found, most households start a search: the drawer, the cupboard, a bag in the closet.

A home money corner turns that search into a thirty-second reach.

## What it is

One dedicated place, a shelf, a box or a small cabinet, where all important financial and personal documents live. Not a pile. A system.

## What goes in

**Insurance:** policies, receipts, beneficiary forms and the contact for claims.

**Banking:** account details (not passwords), statements you need to keep, loan papers.

**Property and vehicles:** titles, contracts, registration.

**Identity and family:** copies of IDs, birth and marriage certificates.

**Taxes and employment:** contracts, certificates and records.

**Medical:** important records, prescriptions and HMO details.

## A simple structure

- **A folder per category,** clearly labelled.
- **A "to do" folder** for items that need action.
- **A one-page index** on top: what exists, where it is, and who to call.

## Keep digital copies

Scan or photograph the key documents and store them securely, with a trusted backup. Remember to keep passwords in a proper password manager, not in the folders.

## Tell someone

Make sure at least one trusted person knows where the corner is and how to open it. If something happens to you, they won't have to guess.

## Maintain it in ten minutes a month

File new papers. Remove expired ones. Check the index.

## Do it this weekend

Gather everything in one place. You'll probably find a forgotten policy or a missing document. That's exactly why it's worth doing.
