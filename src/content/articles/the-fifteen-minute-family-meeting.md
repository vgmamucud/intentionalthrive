Households run on a thousand small decisions: who picks up the kids, what's for dinner, which bill is due, what's coming this weekend. Without a shared moment to coordinate, those decisions pile into tension.

A short weekly meeting replaces scattered reminders with one calm conversation.

## The rules

**Fifteen minutes, once a week.** Same day, same time, ideally on a weekend evening.

**Everyone who lives there is invited,** at an age-appropriate level.

**It isn't a complaint session.** The agenda is the week ahead, not the week behind.

## A simple agenda

1. **Highlights (2 minutes).** One good thing each person noticed this week.
2. **The calendar (4 minutes).** Who needs to be where, and who is doing what.
3. **Money (3 minutes).** Anything due or coming up, any decision needed. Keep it light and factual.
4. **Home (3 minutes).** Chores, meals, errands, repairs.
5. **One thing we're looking forward to (2 minutes).** Something small to plan or anticipate.
6. **Thank-yous (1 minute).**

## With children

Give them a role: reading the calendar, choosing a meal, deciding the weekend activity. It makes them feel part of the household and quietly teaches planning.

## With extended family

If you share a home with parents or relatives, include them. Unspoken expectations are the source of many quiet tensions, and a regular, respectful conversation reduces them.

## What to do when it gets heated

Pause, and put the topic on next week's list. A meeting that stays calm is more valuable than one that resolves everything.

## Start this week

Choose the day. Put it on the shared calendar. Write the agenda on a sticky note and keep it simple.

Fifteen minutes a week is a small price for a household that feels like a team.
