Long goals can feel like walking toward a horizon that doesn't get closer. The balance barely changes. The finish line stays distant. Motivation fades.

A small wins ledger tackles that problem directly.

## What it is

A notebook or note where you write down, each week, the small things you did that moved your life forward. It takes three minutes.

## What counts as a win

- Paid a bill on time.
- Made a savings transfer.
- Said no to an impulse purchase.
- Opened and understood a statement.
- Had a hard money conversation.
- Learned one new term.
- Declined a commitment you couldn't hold.

None of these looks dramatic. Together they're the substance of change.

## Why it works

**It records effort, not only outcomes.** You control effort. You don't control markets, timing or other people.

**It counters memory bias.** We remember failures vividly and successes faintly. Writing corrects the balance.

**It builds identity.** Each entry is evidence that you're someone who does these things.

## How to keep it

1. Choose one place: a notebook, your phone's notes.
2. Pick a regular time: Sunday evening works well.
3. Write five lines. Date them.
4. Once a quarter, read the whole thing.

## When you have a bad week

Write the smallest win you can find. "I opened the account." "I thought about it." Honest and small is better than blank.

## What you'll notice

In a few months, a surprising length of record. Progress you couldn't see in the moment becomes visible on the page.

Start this Sunday with five lines. Date the page.
