You set a goal. You worked toward it. Then life shifted, and the goal no longer fits.

Maybe it's a career path you've outgrown, a house you no longer want, a savings target tied to a plan you've dropped. It can feel like failure, or like wasted time.

It isn't.

## Goals are tools

A goal exists to help you build the life you want. When the life changes, the tool may need changing too. Keeping it out of loyalty doesn't serve you.

## Distinguish three situations

**1. Wanting to quit because it's hard.** The goal still matters, and discomfort is the signal to adjust the method, not the destination.

**2. Wanting to change because circumstances shifted.** An illness, a new child, a changed income. Adaptation is sensible.

**3. Wanting to change because you've changed.** Your values moved. The goal no longer reflects you.

Be honest about which one applies.

## How to update a plan

1. **Name what's changed.** In one sentence.
2. **Review what you've built.** The savings, skills and habits are yours regardless.
3. **Write the new goal.** Specific, with a date and a number.
4. **Redirect the resources.** Move savings or time intentionally, not by drift.
5. **Check your protection.** New goals often mean new responsibilities. Review coverage, beneficiaries and your emergency fund.

## Release the story

You don't owe anyone an explanation for growing. Share what you want to, with the people who matter, and let the rest go.

## Beware the drift

The danger isn't changing goals. It's letting them lapse without choosing a new one. Drift costs years.

## Try this

Write down one goal you've been carrying out of habit. Ask which of the three situations applies, and decide: keep, adjust or retire.

A plan that can change is a plan you'll actually keep.
