Many of us take days off in name only. We check messages, answer one email, plan the next week in our heads and call it rest.

A real day off leaves you restored. It's worth protecting like any other priority.

## What makes it real

**You're not on call.** Work messages are off, or at least not checked.

**You aren't catching up.** It isn't a day for the errands pile or the household backlog.

**It includes something you enjoy,** not just recovery from exhaustion.

## Set it up in advance

Rest rarely happens by accident. A little preparation helps:

- **Choose the day** and put it in the calendar.
- **Tell people:** colleagues, clients, family. A short message the day before sets expectations.
- **Handle urgent items** the day before, so nothing hangs over you.
- **Plan one pleasure,** a meal, a walk, a hobby, a visit.

## Deal with the guilt

Guilt arrives, especially when others rely on you. Remind yourself: rested people make better decisions, treat others better and last longer. Rest isn't taken from your responsibilities. It supports them.

## For households that need you every day

If a full day isn't possible, create a protected half day, or a few hours, and be consistent. Ask your partner to take over for that window, and offer the same in return.

## Make it affordable

Rest doesn't need to be expensive. A day at home, a park, a long meal with family and no screens can do more than a costly trip. If you do want a bigger rest, plan for it with a small monthly fund so it doesn't strain your budget.

## Try this

Choose one day in the next two weeks. Write "off" in the calendar. Tell one person. Notice what you do with your attention that day.

Rest isn't the opposite of progress. It's part of how progress lasts.
