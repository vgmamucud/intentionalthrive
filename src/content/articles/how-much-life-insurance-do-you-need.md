"How much coverage should I get?" is the question I hear most, and the honest answer is: it depends on what you're protecting.

Rules of thumb give a number fast. They don't tell you whether it's right for your life. A better approach is to build the number from what your family would actually need if your income stopped.

## Part 1: Replace the income

How many years would your family need your income to continue while they adjust? Until the children are independent? Until a spouse can return to work? Multiply your annual contribution to the household by that number of years.

## Part 2: Clear the debts

Add what you owe that would pass to someone else: housing loan, car loan, cards, personal loans, and any informal loans. These shouldn't become your family's problem on the worst day of their lives.

## Part 3: Fund the goals

Add the future costs you've already promised yourself: education for your children, support for your parents, any commitments you'd want honoured.

## Part 4: Cover final expenses

A funeral and settling affairs come with immediate bills. Add a reasonable amount so nobody has to borrow in the first week.

## Then subtract what exists

Take away what's already in place: existing life insurance, group cover from your employer (which usually ends if you leave), and savings you'd be willing to use. What's left is your gap.

## Two honest cautions

**The answer is a range, not a number.** Run a conservative and a comfortable version.

**It must be payable.** The right coverage is the one you can keep paying in your tightest month. A smaller plan you hold beats a larger one that lapses. Term insurance often delivers the most protection per peso for a defined period.

## Do it this week

Write the four parts on a page. Total them. Subtract what you have. Take that number to an advisor and ask them to show you the cost of covering it in two ways: term and permanent.

You'll be shopping for an answer you've already worked out, which is the best position to buy from.
