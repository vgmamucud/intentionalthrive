If you work from home, or your phone is also your office, the day has no natural end. Tasks follow you into dinner, into the bedroom, into your head at 2 a.m.

A shutdown ritual gives the day a clear edge.

## Why an unfinished day keeps running

The mind tends to hold on to open loops, the things we meant to do or haven't decided. Without a place to put them, they keep circling.

A shutdown ritual gives them somewhere to go.

## A ten-minute version

**1. Capture (3 minutes).** Write every open item on one list. Don't solve them. Just get them out of your head.

**2. Check tomorrow (3 minutes).** Open the calendar. Notice the first commitment and anything that needs preparation.

**3. Choose the three (2 minutes).** Pick the three most important things for tomorrow. Write them at the top of the page.

**4. A quick money glance (1 minute).** Anything due or arriving? A payment, a reply, a decision.

**5. Say the line (1 minute).** A short phrase that signals "done." For example: *"That's enough for today."* Close the laptop.

## Create a physical edge

- Close the laptop and put it away.
- Put the work phone in a drawer, or silence notifications.
- Change something: lights, music, clothes or a short walk.

These small cues tell your body the shift has happened.

## When the day goes badly

Do it anyway, even in a shorter form. The ritual matters most on messy days, because that's when the open loops multiply.

## What improves

Better sleep, fewer evening interruptions and more presence with the people at home.

Try it for five evenings and see what shifts. The day is allowed to end.
