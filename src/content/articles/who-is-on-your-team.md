We tell stories of people who built things on their own. Look closer, and nearly every plan has people quietly holding it up.

Your financial life, too, works better with a team. Who's on yours?

## The roles worth filling

**A listener.** Someone you can tell the truth to about money without being judged.

**A peer.** Someone at a similar stage, who shares what they're learning.

**A guide.** Someone a few steps ahead who has made mistakes you can learn from.

**A professional.** A licensed advisor, accountant or lawyer who handles specifics, with clear credentials and transparent fees.

**A partner in the plan.** Your spouse, a sibling or a friend who shares goals and holds you to them.

## Look at the gaps

Write the roles and the names. Where is the page empty? For many of us, it's the "listener" and the "guide."

## How to build it

- **Ask a specific question** of someone you trust: "How do you handle this?"
- **Join or form a small group** that meets regularly to share goals.
- **Choose your professionals deliberately.** Check licenses, ask how they're paid and notice how they listen.
- **Offer help first.** Teams are built by giving, not only asking.

## Protect the relationships

Money can strain friendships. Be clear on expectations, keep promises and avoid mixing loans with closeness unless you're prepared for the consequences.

## Let them see the plan

A team can only help with what it knows. Share your goals and a simple version of your plan with the people who matter.

## Be on someone else's team

When you find your footing, make room for someone behind you. It's one of the best ways to consolidate what you've learned.

Write the five roles tonight, put a name next to each and message one person this week.
