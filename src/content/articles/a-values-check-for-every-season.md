The things that mattered most at twenty-five may not be the things that matter at forty. Yet many of us run our finances on values we set once and never revisited.

A values check is a short, honest conversation with yourself.

## Why seasons change values

A new baby, a bigger role, a loss, a health scare, a move: each can shift what you want most. A plan built for a different season can leave you working hard toward something that no longer fits.

## The check

Set aside forty-five minutes once a year, and again after a major change.

**1. Write what mattered most last year.** Honestly, not aspirationally.

**2. Write what matters most now.** Does the list look the same?

**3. Look at where money and time went.** Bank statements and calendars don't lie.

**4. Mark the mismatches.** Where does your spending or time contradict your stated values?

**5. Decide one adjustment.** Not five. One change in money and one in time.

## Questions that help

- What am I tired of doing?
- What would I regret not doing?
- What would I like to stop apologising for?
- What does "enough" mean this year?

## Look at your protection too

Changes in values usually mean changes in responsibilities. New dependents, a different income, a new goal: each may call for a review of coverage, savings and beneficiaries.

## Share it

If you have a partner, do the check separately and compare notes. Differences become conversations, not conflicts.

## Keep it light

You aren't passing judgement on past choices. You're giving the next season a chance to be intentional.

Put an hour in your calendar for your birthday or the new year. Bring a notebook.
